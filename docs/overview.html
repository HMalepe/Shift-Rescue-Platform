The problem

Relief cover is a high-stakes scheduling job run on informal tools.

A pharmacy legally cannot trade without a responsible pharmacist on the floor. When one is sick, on leave, or resigns, the manager has hours — sometimes minutes — to find a registered replacement.

Today that happens through WhatsApp groups and personal contacts. Which means:

  • No verification. The manager is trusting a screenshot of a registration certificate, if they ask for one at all.
  • No reliability history. A locum who no-showed at another branch last month arrives with a clean slate.
  • No distance logic. The group is broadcast to everyone; the person 40 km away sees it at the same time as the person around the corner.
  • No record. Who actually worked which hours becomes an argument at month-end.
  • No control over who sees a post. "Looking for a locum" broadcasts to an entire group whether the manager wants that visibility or not.
  • Personal numbers get exposed. Booking through WhatsApp usually means the locum ends up with the manager's personal number, not a business line.
How it works

Two sides, one booking.

Post a shift, get a verified locum

For pharmacy managers
  • Post a shift, or flip a "looking for a locum" switch that pushes it straight to your saved regulars first
  • See registration status, distance and reliability history before you accept
  • Confirm in one tap — the shift is locked to one person, no double-booking
  • Check-in and check-out gives you an attendance record instead of a memory
  • A clean record of hours worked to hand to payroll, instead of reconstructing it from memory

Get found for work near you

For locum pharmacists
  • Set your availability once; get notified when nearby pharmacies need cover
  • Verify your SAPC registration and employment documents once, reuse it everywhere
  • Build a reliability rating that travels with you and gets you booked first
  • See rates and location up front, before accepting
  • Your own record of every shift worked and hours logged, in one place
Why this is hard to copy

The defensibility isn't the app. It's the verified supply and the behaviour record.

  • Verified supply. A pool of pharmacists whose registration and employment documents have actually been checked is slow to build and hard to replicate. It's also the thing a manager is really buying.
  • Reliability data. Every completed shift, cancellation and no-show accumulates. After a year, the platform knows something no WhatsApp group can: who actually turns up.
  • Attendance verification. Opt-in check-in/check-out with location signals turns "I was there" into a record both sides can rely on.
  • Proximity matching. Shifts route by distance and reliability rather than being broadcast to everyone, so good locums aren't buried in noise.
  • Visibility is the manager's choice. A shift can go to saved regulars only, or to a private radius — not to a group of people the manager never chose to broadcast to.
  • No personal numbers exchanged. All messaging runs through the platform's own business number, not the manager's or the locum's personal phone.
  • Density compounds. Once enough pharmacies and locums in one metro are on it, being off it costs you shifts.
How it makes money

One small monthly fee. That's the whole model.

R___/month Per pharmacy · everything included

Access to the verified pool, posting, matching, scheduling and attendance records. No fee per booking, no cut of anyone's rate, no surprise invoice.

One exception, and it's about accountability rather than revenue: cancel a confirmed shift with less than 24 hours' notice and R10 is added to that month's subscription. Small enough that nobody feels robbed, real enough that late cancellations carry a consequence — which is the entire point, since a late cancellation leaves a pharmacy unable to trade.

Locum wages are not our business. The platform never holds, routes, or takes a cut of what a pharmacy pays a locum. That's handled by each pharmacy's own payroll or HR, exactly as it is today. We provide the record of hours worked; the money stays between employer and locum.

Where it starts

One metro, both sides, hand-built.

Marketplaces die from thin liquidity, not from bad software. So the launch is deliberately narrow: Johannesburg and greater Gauteng only, until both sides are dense enough that a posted shift reliably gets filled.

  • Recruit and verify a founding pool of locum pharmacists before any pharmacy is asked to rely on it
  • Hand-match the first pharmacies personally — concierge, not self-serve — to learn what actually breaks
  • Only open self-serve once shifts fill without intervention
Build sequence

Three phases, roughly four months.

PHASE 1

The core booking loop

Profiles, verification, post a shift, accept a shift, check in and out. Nothing else in phase 1. This is the part that has to be boringly reliable.

PHASE 2

Messaging, reminders and collections

WhatsApp notifications for confirmations and reminders, plus subscription billing and the follow-up flow when a pharmacy's card fails.

PHASE 3

Proactive matching

The "looking for a locum" switch that pushes to saved regulars first and widens outward. Held back deliberately until the system is load-tested — it's the one feature designed to fire a burst of notifications at once.

Open questions

What I'm still genuinely unsure about.

Listed deliberately — a plan with no stated uncertainty usually just means the uncertainty hasn't been found yet.

Is a flat monthly fee the right price point?

Working belief: yes — a per-booking fee makes the product feel like it's reaching into your pocket every time you use it, which kills adoption. Flat and small is the bet. What isn't settled is the actual number, and whether it's low enough that a small independent signs up without thinking hard.

Are the existing WhatsApp groups already good enough?

Working belief: not really — six years in retail pharmacy and a year managing this exact bottleneck says the pain is real. Still a lived-experience judgment rather than a measured one; the concierge phase is where it gets tested against other managers' behaviour, not just my own.

What's the actual liquidity threshold for Gauteng?

Working belief: likely higher than the current target in the plan. Not yet re-modelled against that belief — the number in the plan should be treated as provisional until it is.

What does it cost to sign up a pharmacy?

No measured number yet. No paid marketing spend happens until there is one.

Why me

I've worked in retail pharmacy for six years, and I've managed a dispensary for the last year. This isn't a market report — it's the bottleneck I sit inside every week.
